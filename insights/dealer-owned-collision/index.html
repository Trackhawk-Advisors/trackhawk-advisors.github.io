The Dealer-Owned Collision Opportunity

Why Dealers Should Take a Fresh Look at Their Collision Centers

November 2026

Download PDF

Considerations for Dealers Who Own Collision Centers

Is Collision Core to Your Business?

  • Are your collision centers a profit center or a headache?
  • Are you pursuing high-volume insurance Direct Repair Program (DRP) relationships? What is your strategy to grow the collision revenue?
  • Are you investing in your collision business, or could that capital, time and energy be invested elsewhere for a higher return?

What are the Considerations for Selling Collision Centers?

  • Are your collision centers attached to a dealership, or could they be parceled off separately?
  • Could you benefit from a wholesale parts sales agreement with the MSO to supply its stores in that region?
  • What multiple would your franchised store trade for?
    • Would a dealer pay you full value for a collision center?

Why Sell to a Collision Multi-Site Operator (“MSO”)

Strategic Rationale

  • The collision MSOs have a rigorous, formulaic approach to growth and operations. Within 6 months post-acquisition, MSO run-rates are:
  • MSO site-level revenue is > 3x+ the average collision center
  • Avg. Gross and EBITDA margins are ~40% and ~20%, respectively
  • Nearly 100% of MSO volume is driven by insurance DRP or dealer referrals
  • MSOs are experts at throughput and quality, and make the requisite investments in the business to do so

Financial Rationale

  • MSOs pay a premium for standalone shops, but are flexible for arrangements including operating the collision center even if it’s attached to the dealership
  • Owned real estate creates options for the seller: sell it, lease it to the MSO, or negotiate a long-term lease and then sell to a REIT later
  • Dealers can continue supplying parts and retain service revenue after the sale
  • At valuations ~12x+, tuck-in acquisitions are highly accretive to the MSOs
    • MSOs can pay goodwill based on their pro forma, not trailing earnings

Dealer-Owned Collision Centers Are Attractive Acquisition Targets for MSOs

Opportunity for dealer-owned collision centers

  • Of ~40k U.S. collision centers, ~5k+ are viable targets for MSO consolidation
    • Franchised dealers own ~30% of those collision center targets
  • MSOs like dealer-owned collision centers because they are typically large, well-maintained facilities with the requisite OEM special tools and tech training
  • Dealers typically focus on internal demand from their dealerships to drive revenue, whereas the MSOs seek to maximize revenue from DRP relationships
  • There are ~130 active PE firms that have invested >$9bn of capital in the highly fragmented collision sector to drive consolidation

Why Are Collision MSO Valuations So High?

MSOs Benefit From Scale and a High-Margin, Asset-Lite Business Model

  • In the latest landmark transaction, Boyd paid ~13.3x EBITDA ($1.3bn) for Joe Hudson's 258 centers (~9.3x after synergies), setting the value of a scaled network
  • Scale matters in the collision business, and it supports the MSOs’ high margins:
    • Insurers prefer fewer, larger repair partners, and a chain can trade volume for pricing
    • Fixed costs spread out over a larger revenue base + volume discounts on key inputs
  • MSOs are vertically integrated and perform most sublet services in-house (eg, ADAS calibration)
  • By not owning real estate, the MSOs invest cash into acquisitions and driving YoY revenue growth

Illustrative Real Estate Valuation and Considerations for Sellers

  • MSOs generate on avg >3x more revenue than single-site operations (“SSOs”), and the store-level profitability can support higher rents for real estate investors
  • Strong demand from real estate investors, particularly REITs, for MSO-leased collision properties has driven down cap rates to 5.5% to 6.5%+ levels
  • Investor demand is driven by higher quality MSO tenants that provide a corporate guarantee, and sign 15+ year, NNN leases for nearly all their locations
  • Single-site or subscale operators are not likely to find a deep market from investors for a sale-leaseback, given the lower credit quality without a meaningful guarantee
  • The chart illustrates how the real estate is worth more when there is a high-quality MSO tenant (ie, lower cap rate effect) that can comfortably bear more rent expense vs SSO (higher rent effect)

Case Study: Caliber and Crash Champions Acquire Key Collision

Transaction Summary

Sale of Key Auto Group’s Collision Platform

  • Key Auto Group, a franchised car dealer with 23 dealerships across 7 states, established an independently operated collision center platform with 12 locations – 10 in New England, 1 in MS, and 1 in TN
  • Each Key Collision site was a standalone facility on its own parcel, with the real estate owned by Key Auto Group
  • In 2 separate transactions in Aug/Sept-26, 10 Key Collision shops in New England were sold to Caliber Collision, and 1 shop in Mississippi was sold to Crash Champions (Key retained 1 shop in Memphis)

Buyers

  • Caliber: Largest collision repair operator in the U.S., with 1,850+ locations in 44 states
    • Acquired by Hellman & Friedman in 2019 and merged Caliber with ABRA Auto Body
  • Crash Champions: 3rd largest collision repair operator in the U.S., with 650+ locations in 38 states
    • Backed by Clearlake Capital since 2022 and merged with Service King

Transaction Takeaways

  • The Key Collision centers were an attractive regional platform to the MSOs and traded at premium 10x+ Purchase Price / EBITDA valuation multiple and entered into long-term leases with Crash and Caliber for the real estate
    • With high-quality tenants in long-term leases, Key has the option to sell the real estate to a REIT in the future
    • MSO-leased collision properties typically sell to investors at 5.5% to 6.5% cap rates
  • Large, standalone, I-CAR Gold-certified facilities averaging ~18k sqft were instrumental in a higher valuation
    • Well-equipped shops with financials separate from the dealership required minimal capital investment
  • Despite NH, VT, and ME having relatively low population densities (ranked 21st, 31st, and 38th, respectively) these locations present attractive opportunities for MSOs because of lower competitor concentration
