Carvana’s Resurgence

Carvana’s Turnaround from a $1bn Adj. EBITDA Loss to $2.6bn of Adj. EBITDA

2Q 2026

Download PDF

  • 2Q26 results were the best quarterly results in company history in terms of scale and profitability
    • Used retail unit sales of 197,325 +38% YoY
    • Revenue of $7.4bn +52% YoY
    • Adj. EBITDA of $769mm +28.0% YoY and Adj. EBITDA margin of 10.4%
  • CVNA’s 1H26 results show sequential increases in retail units and Adj. EBITDA for new quarterly records; management guided to FY26 Adj. EBITDA of $2.7 – $3.0bn (vs. $2.24bn in FY25)
  • Update on Management Objective:
    • CVNA reaffirmed that it is on the path toward its goal of “selling 3mm retail units per year at a 13.5% Adj. EBITDA margin by 2030 to 2035”
  • With a market cap ~2.1x larger than all the franchised dealers combined and 8.8x KMX, equity investors are “all-in” with CVNA’s expectations that it can expand margins while growing its unit sales over 4x

Source: Publicly available information from company financial statements

(1) Public Dealers includes the combined totals of ABG, AN, GPI, LAD, PAG and SAH. Financial data as of 6/30/26

“Worst to First” - Carvana is now the Most Profitable of all Public Dealers and by Far the Most Valuable

Commentary on CVNA’s Valuation

  • Why is CVNA’s market cap 2.1x and 1.7x larger than that of the franchised and public dealers, respectively?
    • CVNA is a high-profile, e-commerce “tech stock” that is covered by Technology (not auto) equity research analysts
    • CVNA’s avg daily trading volume is >3m shares, while the franchised dealers average <300k shares
    • AMassive organic growth potential selling DTC in a large, highly-fragmented market, while growth for franchised dealers is largely limited to capital-intensive acquisitions
    • B“Asset-lite” business model that requires limited capex and minimal facility overhead
  • What are investors overlooking about CVNA and franchised dealers that creates such a valuation gap?
    • CVNA is entirely exposed to the conditions in the used market
    • Adverse conditions in the securitization market could freeze Carvana’s ability to unload consumer vehicle loans it made
    • Dealers take low / no risk to generate F&I income
    • CVNA is precariously dependent on Ally for floorplan lending and as a buyer for its auto finance receivables
    • Dealers have assets besides cars to pledge as collateral for lenders, as well as support from captives
    • Governance - the Garcias hold >80% voting rights
  • Since 3Q24, U.S. public franchised dealers spent $6.7bn on M&A and $3.4bn on capex ($10.0bn total), while LTM Adj. EBITDA declined ~$216mm
  • Conversely, since 3Q24, CVNA spent $500mm on M&A and capex, while its LTM Adj. EBITDA increased by $1.8bn to $2.6bn
  • CVNA management set a high bar for its future performance
    • Any unusual selling of stock by the Garcias would be a sign that the bar may be too high
