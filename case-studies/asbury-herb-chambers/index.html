Asbury’s Acquisition of Herb Chambers

On 7/21/25, Asbury closed the acquisition of The Herb Chambers Companies

February 2025

Download PDF

Deal Summary

  • On 7/21/25, Asbury closed the acquisition of The Herb Chambers Companies
  • Herb Chambers Highlights:
    • 17th largest private dealer group in the U.S. by revenue (Automotive News Top 150 Dealership Groups, 2025 edition)
    • 2024 Revenue and Adj. EBITDA of $3.2bn and $168mm, respectively
    • 33 dealerships, 52 franchises, and 3 collision centers
    • 50,000 new and used vehicles sold in 2024

Valuation & Investment Rationale

  • Modest blue sky multiple for a luxury-heavy metro platform:
    • Asbury bought a platform at close to the price of individual stores, with little premium for scale
    • The luxury and import brand mix brings more resilient margins, a stronger service business and more affluent customers
  • Asbury's digital retail tools, purchasing scale and back-office consolidation give it room to cut costs and improve margins
  • Asbury paid entirely with cash and debt, issuing no new shares, so all the earnings gain goes to existing shareholders

Acquisition Financing

  • Asbury funded the Herb Chambers acquisition with an upsize to its credit facilities, mortgage proceeds and cash
    • Upsize to Syndicated Credit Facilities:
      • $750mm total
        • $425mm to the Revolver
        • $325mm to the New Vehicle Floorplan
    • Mortgage Proceeds: New $547mm syndicated real estate facility
      • Maturity: 10 years
      • Pricing: SOFR + 200bps
      • Administrative agent: Wells Fargo
  • Observations on ABG’s Lenders Supporting the Acquisition
    • 12 of the 17 lenders provided incremental commitments
      • The top 5 largest lenders to ABG increased by 25-30%
      • Lenders that did not participate in the upsize:
        • Banks: JPM, U.S. Bank and TD Bank
        • Captives: Hyundai and Nissan
